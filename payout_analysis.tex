\section*{Payment rule}
Each participant receives a baseline of \euro{5} plus a bonus of 5\% of their
realized profit (if positive), computed after the demand draw:
\begin{align*}
\pi_S &= w \cdot \min(q, D) - PC \cdot q, &
\pi_R &= (RP_{\mathit{eff}} - w) \cdot \min(q, D),
\end{align*}
with $PC=1$, $RP \in \{4,5\}$, $w \in [1,5]$, $q \le 100$, and
$RP_{\mathit{eff}} = RP + 0.02$ per passed slider (capped at $+1$).
Demand $D$ is the \emph{average of ten} draws from $U[0,100]$, so
$D \approx 50 \pm 9$; extreme demand is effectively impossible.

\section*{Bounds}
\begin{table}[h]\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
 & Min & Theoretical max & Realistic max \\
\midrule
Supplier   & \euro{5} & \euro{25} & $\approx$\,\euro{14} \\
Retailer   & \euro{5} & \euro{30} & $\approx$\,\euro{18} \\
Per pair   & \euro{10} & \euro{35} & $\approx$\,\euro{28} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The minimum is always the baseline (negative profit earns no bonus). The
theoretical maxima require $D=100$, i.e.\ all ten draws equal to 100
(probability $\sim 10^{-20}$); the ten-draw average therefore insures the
budget against extreme payouts. In a 200{,}000-participant simulation the
largest observed payment was \euro{18.09} (95th percentile: \euro{12.16}).

\section*{Expected payouts (90\% agreement rate)}
Monte Carlo with deals at or dispersed around the Nash terms
(RP\,$=4$: $2.80$\euro{}/75; RP\,$=5$: $3.33$\euro{}/80), 10\% of
participants at baseline only:

\begin{table}[h]\centering
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Sliders passed (quality checks) & Supplier & Retailer \\
\midrule
None    & \euro{8.79} & \euro{8.60} \\
Half    & \euro{8.98} & \euro{9.82} \\
All 50  & \euro{8.98} & \euro{11.05} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent\textbf{Central estimate:} $\approx$\,\euro{8.90} per participant
(median \euro{9.10}), i.e.\ $\approx$\,\euro{17.85} per two-human pair.
Budget rule of thumb: \euro{9} per head, p95 $\approx$ \euro{12},
practical ceiling $\approx$ \euro{18}.

\section*{Sensitivities}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Slider effort} is the main lever: each passed slider adds
        $\approx$\,\euro{0.05} to the Retailer's payment (full effort:
        $+$\euro{2.5}), while paying the Supplier nothing.
  \item \textbf{Supplier payouts are flat} ($\approx$\,\euro{8.8--9.0})
        across bargaining outcomes near the Nash terms.
  \item \textbf{AI-retailer sessions} pay only the human Supplier:
        $\approx$\,\euro{8.60} per participant all-in.
\end{itemize}
